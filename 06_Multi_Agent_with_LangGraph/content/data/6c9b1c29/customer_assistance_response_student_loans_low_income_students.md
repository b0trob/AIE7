**Overview of Student Loan Options:**
For low-income students, there are primarily two types of student loans available: **federal student loans** and **private student loans**. Federal loans, such as Direct Subsidized and Unsubsidized Loans, are designed specifically for students with demonstrated financial need. These loans typically have lower interest rates and more flexible repayment options compared to private loans, making them a viable choice for low-income borrowers.

For instance, **Direct Subsidized Loans** do not accrue interest while students are still enrolled in school, significantly lessening the financial burden during their studies. On the other hand, **Direct Unsubsidized Loans** are accessible regardless of need, but interest starts accruing immediately. Understanding the nuances of these options is essential for students to evaluate their financial aid wisely.

**Income-Driven Repayment Plans:**
For existing borrowers, income-driven repayment plans, such as the Income-Sensitive Repayment Plan, offer substantial benefits. These plans adjust monthly payments based on the borrower’s income, ensuring that repayments remain manageable. Recent legislative changes have introduced new options, including the Repayment Assistance Plan, which provides capped minimum payments, though it could still impose a financial strain on the lowest-income borrowers.

**Grants and Additional Resources:**
In addition to loans, low-income students should explore grant opportunities, which do not require repayment and can significantly alleviate the financial stress of pursuing higher education. Completing the FAFSA (Free Application for Federal Student Aid) is advisable to assess eligibility for various grants and subsidized loans.

**Conclusion:**
In conclusion, while student loans are often essential for funding education, low-income students are encouraged to prioritize federal loans and investigate additional financial aid options, including grants, to minimize their post-graduation debt burden.
